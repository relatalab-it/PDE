Space for Smart Tourism — a catalogue-based prototype

Two use cases built from the Italian national catalogue of geographic metadata (RNDT) with openrndt: beach tourism in Rimini and trails in two Piedmont parks.

Prototype. Built by RELATA Lab to explore the ESA Kick-Start call «Space for Smart Tourism». It is not a product and it is not a customer case. Every layer comes from a public record; limits are on the «Method & limits» tab.

How to read the map

A prototype to explore the ESA call «Space for Smart Tourism». It shows what public records say; it does not assess, rank or predict anything.

The basics

  • Tabs: A (Rimini coast), B (Piedmont parks), Method & limits (sources, checks, caveats).
  • Move the map by dragging; zoom with +/−, the mouse wheel or a pinch.
  • Layers: tick a box to show a layer. The round i next to a layer opens its record: body, licence, date of the data, link to the national catalogue (RNDT), the service it was read from and the download date.
  • Hover (or tap) a line or a dot to read its label.

Tab A — Coast and beach tourism, Rimini

What you see

  • Emerged beach 2023 (sand colour): the beach as mapped by Regione Emilia-Romagna.
  • Coastlines 2000, 2006, 2020: three national ISPRA lines. The 2020 line is coloured by ISPRA's «coastal dynamics»: red = erosion, green = advance, grey = stability, light grey = no information.
  • Susceptibility (off by default): colours drawn by the Region's own service (link to its legend under the layers).
  • Yellow dots: the 232 beach establishments listed by the Comune di Rimini.

What to do

  • Zoom into a stretch of coast and switch the 2000, 2006 and 2020 lines on together: where they separate, the shoreline moved.
  • Hover a 2020 segment to read its class; hover a dot to see the establishment and its zone.
  • Read the left panel: kilometres of coast per class, and how many establishments sit next to each class.

What it means — and what it does not

  • «Erosion» and «advance» describe the 2006–2020 change according to ISPRA's record. An advance can also come from nourishment or defence works.
  • The count of establishments per class uses the nearest 2020 segment within 250 m. It is indicative: not a risk assessment, not a forecast.
  • The lines come from aerial orthophotos, not satellites. No satellite data is used in this tab; adding Sentinel-2 is a to-do.

Tab B — Parks and trails, Piedmont

What you see

  • Colour mosaic: Sentinel-2 satellite data processed by ARPA Piemonte. Each pixel mixes the vegetation index (NDVI) of three seasons: red = autumn, green = spring, blue = summer. Whitish or bright: vegetation active in all three seasons. Dark grey to black: little vegetation (rock, snow, water, built-up). A strong single colour: vegetation active mostly in one season.
  • White line: the protected area. Yellow: the regional trail network of Piedmont (coarse, about 1:250,000). Orange (Gran Paradiso only): the trails of Valle d'Aosta (detailed, 1:10,000).
  • The mosaics exist only for Piedmont, so the Valle d'Aosta side of the Gran Paradiso is blank.

What to do

  • Choose a park in the menu. With Compare two periods on, drag the slider: left is 2020–21, right is 2022–23. Switch it off for a single view and use Opacity to see the base map underneath.
  • Follow a trail across the colours to see which kinds of ground it crosses.

What it means — and what it does not

  • It shows vegetation cycles and land cover. It is not a map of vegetation health, trampling or visitor pressure, and there are no visitor counts here.
  • The two mosaics can differ in tone because of which images were available or how each was processed, so differences are not proof of change on the ground.
  • The yellow and orange trail files have different detail and are not a complete survey.

If something looks wrong

Open «Method & limits»: it lists the checks made on projections and positions, and the known limits.

What this is

A small web map showing that an AI agent can compose queries to the official RNDT catalogue (through openrndt) and return real, checkable records, service URLs and licences, instead of inventing them. The layers below were downloaded read-only on the date shown. Rasters are snapshots of the services on that date; vectors are simplified copies (2–20 m) for display, not the original data.

Checks made on positions and projections (06/11/2026)

  • ISPRA coastlines and Rimini establishments: the same service requested in WGS84 and in the native UTM 33N gives identical coordinates (difference 0.00 m).
  • Park outlines (shapefile, UTM 32N) against the Regione Piemonte WFS service: Gran Paradiso overlap 99.9% (710.2 km² on both); Monviso 94.6% (the service lists adjacent areas and its outline is larger: 96.4 km² against 91.5 km²).
  • Piedmont trails against OpenStreetMap paths: 86% of the length in Monviso and 77% in Gran Paradiso fall within 100 m of an OSM path. The file's own scale is 1:250,000, so offsets of tens of metres are expected.
  • Valle d'Aosta trails: the file is in ED50 / UTM 32N, not WGS84. Read with the declared projection, 99% of the length falls within 50 m of an OSM path; read as if it were WGS84, only 34%. The projection was therefore applied from the file's own definition.
  • Sentinel-2 mosaics: the tiles use the standard Web Mercator scheme (same origin and tile size as web maps). Moving OSM main roads east-west or north-south by up to 300 m, the image is darkest along the roads exactly at 0 m offset, for both parks and both periods (so there is no visible shift).
  • Regione Emilia-Romagna beach and susceptibility images: 99% of the 232 establishments lie within about 23 m of a drawn pixel of the 2023 beach layer, and 87% of the 2020 coastline points within about 116 m (the rest are river mouths, ports and works with no beach).
  • These are consistency checks against independent sources, not a survey-grade accuracy assessment. Rasters are shown in Web Mercator, as the web map does.

Limits

  • Case A has no satellite data. The ISPRA coastlines come from orthophotos (2017–2020 for the 2020 version), not from satellites.
  • The ISPRA «coastal dynamics» class (2020 v2.0) describes the 2006–2020 evolution according to the dataset's own record. The classes of the 2000 and 2006 files are not comparable with it. The count of establishments near each class is indicative and is not a risk assessment.
  • Case B shows seasonal NDVI mosaics for two periods: they indicate vegetation cycles and land-cover change, not vegetation health, not trampling and not visitor pressure. There are no visitor counts in the catalogue data used here.
  • The two mosaics differ strongly in overall tone (in the Monviso area 2020–21 is much darker). This may reflect which seasonal images were available or how each mosaic was processed rather than change on the ground. No radiometric comparability check was made, so the swipe is a visual comparison only.
  • Older ARPA Piemonte mosaics (2016–2020) answered «Token required» when the tiles were requested on 06/11/2026, so only the 2020–21 and 2022–23 mosaics are shown.
  • The Piedmont trail file is the regional landscape-plan network (main and secondary routes, scale 1:250,000, files dated 2013–2016): coarse and not a complete survey. The Valle d'Aosta cadastre (1:10,000, published trails only) is far more detailed, so trail lengths of the two regions must not be compared.
  • The satellite mosaics are shown only inside the Piedmont footprint of the service (the province polygons published with it) and are transparent elsewhere, so both periods cover the same area. The Valle d'Aosta side of the Gran Paradiso therefore has no imagery.
  • Records and licences were read from the RNDT on the download date. For the two Regione Piemonte records, the normalised licence field is empty and the CC BY 4.0 condition was read from the record's use-conditions field.
  • The establishments file for Rimini was not reachable from the RNDT record (no link). It was found on the Comune's open-data page and read from its public service; only an identifier and the beach zone are kept.
